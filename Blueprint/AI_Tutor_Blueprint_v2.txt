AI-INTEGRATED LEARNING SYSTEM FOR NIGERIAN SCHOOLS
Blueprint v0.2 — Concept Stage, Viability Analysis Added
Author: Eeyung Emmanuel
Date: August 2026

===================================================
1. THE PROBLEM
===================================================
Most Nigerian secondary and upper-primary students learn from teachers who
are themselves not always well-informed on the concepts they are teaching.
This creates a compounding gap: the student doesn't understand the topic,
and the person meant to fix that misunderstanding often can't, either.

Existing AI tutoring tools (e.g. Metaschool AI, GRIO, the World Bank/Microsoft
Copilot pilot in Edo State) have already proven that AI tutoring works in
this exact context — a 2024 Edo State pilot measured close to two years of
learning gain in six weeks. So the core idea is validated. This blueprint is
not trying to prove AI tutoring works. It's trying to build a version that:
  (a) diagnoses what a student actually knows before teaching anything,
  (b) compensates for teacher knowledge gaps, not just student ones, and
  (c) survives real Nigerian connectivity and data conditions.

===================================================
2. TARGET USERS
===================================================
- Primary 4 – Primary 6 (roughly ages 9–11)
- JSS1 – JSS3 (Junior Secondary, roughly ages 12–14)
- SSS1 – SSS3 (Senior Secondary, roughly ages 15–17)

These are three very different cognitive and literacy levels. The system
must NOT use one voice for all of them. Language complexity, sentence
length, and abstraction level need to scale by band. A concept explained to
a Primary 5 student and the same concept explained to an SSS3 student should
read like two different tutors, even if the underlying idea is the same.

===================================================
3. SCOPE OF SUBJECT MATTER
===================================================
"Should be able to explain any topic" — for this to be honest rather than
aspirational, the system needs to be subject-AGNOSTIC in method, not
subject-agnostic in content accuracy. The interaction pattern (diagnose,
fill gap, hands-on practice) is reusable across any subject. The accuracy
of what's taught still depends on the underlying model's actual knowledge
of that specific topic, which varies by subject and curriculum depth.

Realistic phased subject coverage:
  Phase 1 (proof of concept): One subject you know deeply from your own
  teaching background — Mathematics or Physics — at one band (start with
  JSS or SSS, since you have direct teaching experience there).
  Phase 2: Expand to Basic Science / Core subjects across bands.
  Phase 3: Broaden to the full NERDC/WAEC curriculum spread.

Do not attempt "any topic, any band" as the first build. That's the
long-term claim, not the starting scope.

===================================================
4. CORE INTERACTION PATTERN
===================================================
This reuses the same Socratic, one-concept-at-a-time pattern from your
Engineering Mentor Prompt (already validated, 9.6/10, used across your
cohort) — retargeted from software concepts to school curriculum concepts.

Four-stage loop per concept:

  STAGE 1 — DIAGNOSE
  Ask the student to explain, in their own words, what they already know
  about the topic before teaching anything. Use their answer to locate the
  actual gap, not an assumed one. Two students asking about "fractions"
  may have completely different gaps — one may not understand division as
  a concept at all; another may understand division but not why a fraction
  represents "the same thing done differently."

  STAGE 2 — FILL THE SPECIFIC GAP
  Teach only the piece that's actually missing, using an explanation style
  suited to the student's grade band. Avoid re-teaching what the student
  already demonstrated they know in Stage 1.

  STAGE 3 — HANDS-ON APPLICATION
  Give the student a small, concrete task or problem to apply the concept
  themselves, not just observe an explanation. This is the "hands-on
  learning" piece — the model should not consider a concept taught until
  the student has done something with it.

  STAGE 4 — VERIFY AND CLOSE THE LOOP
  Check whether the application attempt reveals a remaining
  misunderstanding. If yes, loop back to Stage 2 for that specific piece
  only. If no, briefly confirm mastery and stop — do not over-explain a
  concept the student has already shown they understand.

===================================================
5. AGE-BAND CALIBRATION RULES
===================================================
- Primary 4–6: short sentences, concrete real-world analogies (objects,
  money, food, familiar Nigerian contexts), no abstract terminology without
  immediately grounding it in an example.
- JSS1–3: can introduce proper terminology, but must still pair every new
  term with a plain-language explanation the first time it's used.
- SSS1–3: can use full technical/curriculum vocabulary, multi-step
  reasoning, and reference exam-style question formats (WAEC/NECO/JAMB).

===================================================
6. DESIGN CONSTRAINT: LOW-DATA, LOW-POWER FIRST
===================================================
This is the one advantage a well-funded competitor is less likely to have
prioritized: you are currently living the exact conditions the target
users face — unreliable power, expensive or scarce data, phone-only access.
Design requirements that follow directly from that:
  - Text-first interaction. No reliance on images, audio, or video unless
    explicitly necessary for the concept (e.g. a diagram-dependent topic).
  - Short exchanges. A student should be able to complete one full
    diagnose-teach-practice-verify loop in a handful of short messages,
    not a long continuous session, so it survives a spotty connection.
  - Resumable sessions. A student should be able to stop mid-concept and
    pick back up without repeating the diagnostic stage.

===================================================
7. KNOWN RISK — DO NOT SKIP
===================================================
Research on the Edo State pilot explicitly noted that AI tutoring used
WITHOUT teacher guidance can create false confidence in students — the
tool can make a student feel they understand something when they don't,
if there's no human checkpoint. Given the whole premise of this system is
partly to compensate for teachers who may not catch that themselves, this
risk needs a real answer, not a footnote. Two options to weigh later:
  (a) Build in a lightweight "teacher view" — a simple summary of what
      gaps were found and closed per student, so a teacher or parent can
      spot-check even without deep subject knowledge themselves.
  (b) Have the system periodically ask the student to re-explain a concept
      days later, to catch false confidence before it hardens.
This should not be treated as optional polish — it's a core design
question, because it's the exact failure mode this system is most likely
to hit at scale.

===================================================
8. VIABILITY ANALYSIS (added — see full reasoning discussed separately)
===================================================

8.1 EVIDENCE VALIDATION — CONFIRMED, WITH ONE IMPORTANT GAP
The Edo State pilot statistic is accurate: independently verified at
roughly 0.29-0.31 standard deviations effect size, described by World Bank
researchers as outperforming ~80% of documented education interventions.
This is real, strong evidence that AI tutoring works in a Nigerian context.

THE GAP NOT TO GLOSS OVER: every version of that proven pilot involved
teachers actively guiding structured, in-lab sessions — not a student using
a tool independently. Researchers (including Wharton's Ethan Mollick)
explicitly flagged that AI tutoring WITHOUT teacher guidance can create
false confidence rather than real learning. This means the evidence cited
in Section 1 validates "AI tutoring + a present, reliable teacher" — it
does NOT yet validate "AI tutoring compensating for an unreliable or
absent teacher," which is this product's actual core premise. That gap is
not proven by the pilot — it is the open question this product is betting
on. Section 7's mitigation ideas (teacher view, periodic re-explanation
checks) are the right instinct; treat closing this specific gap as the
primary goal of the Step 2 manual test (Section 9), not a secondary detail.

8.2 COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE — REAL, BUT DIFFERENTIATED
Metaschool AI is a real, operating Nigerian competitor: "on-demand NECO
revision, JAMB UTME readiness, topic-by-topic analytics and live mentors
for every subject." This is a BREADTH-first product (every subject, every
exam, human mentors layered on AI). This blueprint's actual bet is
DEPTH-first: one subject, one band, a genuine diagnose-before-teach loop,
no human mentor layer required. This is a real, defensible point of
differentiation, not direct head-on competition — but it means this
product should never be pitched as "we're an AI tutor too." The pitch is:
"most tools teach; this one finds out what a specific student actually
doesn't understand before saying anything." Keep this distinction explicit
in any future pitch, grant application, or school conversation.

8.3 PROBLEM VALIDATION — PASSES THE CORE TEST
Private extra lessons ("afternoon lessons"), past-question workbooks, and
home tutors are long-standing, widely-used Nigerian workarounds for this
exact problem. Parents and students already spend real money trying to
close this gap. This is the same validation pattern that separated
Moniepoint's real success from what3words'/Google Plus Codes' failure to
gain adoption (see the separate Product Validation Gut Check reference) —
this product removes friction from and reduces the cost of something
people already do and already pay for, rather than asking anyone to adopt
new behavior. This is a genuine positive signal, not just hopeful framing.

8.4 STILL OPEN — WHO PAYS (determines the shape of everything else)
Three real, different paths, not yet decided:
  - Schools pay (B2B) — slower sales cycle, needs institutional buy-in,
    but stickier revenue and higher per-deal value.
  - Parents pay directly (B2C) — faster to test, but a harder, more
    crowded consumer sale competing directly with Metaschool AI and
    private tutors on price and trust.
  - NGO/government sponsorship (grant-funded) — no direct monetization
    pressure, but dependent on programs and timelines outside your
    control, and typically slower to secure.
This should be decided intentionally, not by default, since it changes
build priorities (a school-facing product needs the "teacher view" from
Section 7 far more urgently than a pure consumer product would).

8.5 FUNDING/ECOSYSTEM CONTEXT (informational, not a current action item)
Meta and Nigeria's Federal Ministry of Communications, Innovation and
Digital Economy launched "AI Academy Nigeria" in August 2026, including a
Startup Pitchathon offering up to $5,000 in equity-free funding for
early-stage Nigerian AI ideas/products — a real, concrete example of
non-dilutive funding interest forming around exactly this category of
product. That specific application window closed August 21, 2026 (link:
https://events.atmeta.com/aiacademynigerialaunch). Worth checking that
same link periodically for future cohorts or the program's other tracks
(AI Skills Development, Developer Bootcamp), rather than treating this as
a one-time closed door — but not an immediate action item at concept
stage.

8.6 OVERALL VIABILITY READ
Stronger and better-grounded than most ideas evaluated so far: real
validated problem, real existing willingness to pay, genuine
differentiation from the nearest competitor, and a founder with direct,
non-transferable subject-matter teaching experience. The one thing not yet
proven is the thing the product depends on most — whether AI alone can
safely close a knowledge gap without a present teacher, without drifting
into false confidence. This is cheap to test (Section 9, Steps 1-2) before
any infrastructure is built, and should be treated as the make-or-break
question, not a footnote.

===================================================
9. BUILD SEQUENCE (MATCHES YOUR EXISTING PLAYBOOK)
===================================================
Same pattern as the three-phase page-building agent system — prove the
prompt before building any infrastructure around it.

  Step 1: Write the actual diagnostic-mentor system prompt for ONE
  concept, in ONE subject, at ONE grade band. No app, no code.
  Step 2: Manually run it — on yourself, a sibling, a student you have
  access to, or even a cohort-mate's younger relative. Does it actually
  diagnose the real gap, or does it just lecture? Specifically test
  whether it holds up WITHOUT a teacher present guiding the session (see
  Section 8.1) — this is the single most important thing to learn from
  this step, more important than whether the explanations are good.
  Step 3: Only after Step 2 shows real signal, consider wrapping it in an
  interface (could reuse your existing Multi-Agent Orchestrator scaffolding
  rather than building new infrastructure from scratch).

===================================================
10. OPEN QUESTIONS (unresolved — revisit later)
===================================================
- Curriculum alignment: NERDC scheme of work vs. WAEC/NECO syllabus —
  which one anchors "correctness" for a given topic?
- Who is the paying or sponsoring user (see Section 8.4 for the three
  real options) — schools, parents, NGOs, or the student directly?
- How does the system handle a topic it genuinely doesn't know well —
  does it say so, or is there a risk of confident wrong answers to a
  9-year-old who has no way to catch the error?
- Does the product need to prove it works WITHOUT teacher guidance to
  fulfill its own premise (Section 8.1), and if Step 2 testing shows it
  can't reliably do that, does the product pivot toward a teacher-assisted
  model instead (closer to the proven Edo pilot shape) rather than a fully
  independent one?
