SAMPLE AI TUTOR LESSON
======================

Purpose
-------
This file contains a cleaned record of the tutoring interaction used to explore
the proposed AI tutor product. It preserves the important interaction patterns,
corrections, and failures observed during the lesson.

PART 1: MATHEMATICS
===================

The tutor practiced simple addition with learners referred to as Favour and
Delight.

Example questions and answers:
- 3 + 4 = 7
- 6 + 2 = 8
- 9 + 1 = 10
- 5 + 2 = 7
- 7 + 3 was initially treated as 11, but was later corrected to 10.
- 4 + 7 + 9 = 20
- 2 + 5 + 1 = 8
- 3 + 4 + 2 was first described as 10, then correctly corrected to 9.
- 6 + 1 + 2 = 9
- 4 + 3 + 1 = 8
- 5 + 2 + 0 = 7

IMPORTANT INTERACTION RULE DISCOVERED
------------------------------------
The tutor was corrected with the following instruction:

"When you ask the equation, wait for them to answer."

The desired pattern was also clarified:
- Ask one learner a question.
- Allow that learner time to answer.
- Do not reveal or supply the answer before the learner responds.
- After the learner gives an answer, ask for the answer again.
- The learner should confirm the answer two times in total before moving on.
- Do not ask for the answer three times.
- Then move to the next question and/or next learner.

Example:
Tutor: "Favour, what is 2 + 5 + 1?"
Learner: "Eight, I think."
Tutor: "Correct. Say 8 again."
Learner: "Eight."
Tutor: Move on to the next learner.

OBSERVED PROBLEMS
-----------------
1. Incorrect validation:
   The tutor sometimes marked an answer correct when it was wrong.

2. Inconsistent arithmetic:
   "7 + 3 = 11" was initially accepted as correct before being corrected to 10.

3. Premature responses:
   The tutor sometimes answered or continued before properly waiting for the
   learner.

4. Repetition confusion:
   The desired rule was two confirmations, but the tutor occasionally asked for
   unnecessary additional repetitions.

5. Answer checking was not enough:
   A learner could repeat the correct answer without demonstrating that they
   understood the concept or method.

PART 2: CHRISTIAN RELIGIOUS STUDIES
===================================

Questions used included:
- Who created the world? Answer: God.
- Who was the first man? Answer: Adam.
- Who built the ark to save his family and the animals from the flood?
  Answer: Noah.
- Who led the Israelites out of Egypt? Answer: Moses.
- Who defeated Goliath? Answer: David.
- How long did it rain during Noah's flood? Answer given: forty days and
  forty nights.
- Who was swallowed by a big fish? Answer: Jonah.
- Who was the mother of Jesus? Answer: Mary.

The same turn-taking rule applies:
- Call a learner by name.
- Ask the question.
- Allow the learner to answer without interruption.
- Evaluate the answer.
- Request one confirmation when appropriate.
- Move on after the required confirmations.
- Do not supply the answer before giving the learner a fair opportunity.

MULTI-LEARNER SESSION BEHAVIOR
==============================

The lesson involved more than one learner, including Favour, Delight, and a
possible additional learner, Precious.

The product may therefore need to support:
- Multiple named learners in one session.
- Individual turns.
- Knowing whose turn it is.
- Preventing another learner from being treated as the respondent.
- Separate progress for each learner.
- Handling interruptions and side comments.
- Moving from one learner to another cleanly.

CORE PRODUCT LESSONS FROM THIS SESSION
======================================

1. The AI needs explicit turn management.
2. The AI must wait for an answer before evaluating.
3. Mathematical answers should be checked reliably.
4. The AI should not confidently approve an answer unless it has verified it.
5. Repetition rules must be tracked by session state.
6. A correct final answer should not automatically mean mastery.
7. The tutor should eventually ask follow-up questions that test understanding
   or transfer, not merely repetition.
8. Each learner should have an individual learning record.
9. The system must handle subject switching, such as Mathematics to Christian
   Religious Studies.
10. Interruptions should not cause the tutor to lose track of the active learner
    or the current question.

RECOMMENDED STATE FLOW FOR A FUTURE PROTOTYPE
=============================================

1. SELECT_LEARNER
2. ASK_QUESTION
3. WAIT_FOR_ANSWER
4. CHECK_ANSWER
5. HANDLE_INCORRECT_OR_UNCERTAIN_ANSWER
6. REQUEST_CONFIRMATION
7. WAIT_FOR_CONFIRMATION
8. VERIFY_UNDERSTANDING
9. RECORD_RESULT
10. MOVE_TO_NEXT_LEARNER_OR_QUESTION

This lesson is a prototype observation record. It is intended to help identify
the behavioral requirements for an AI tutoring product before building the full
application.
